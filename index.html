Kickoff checklist

    Who it's for: the ideal client profile

    Built for one kind of business first, so every feature answers a real problem they have.

    The company

    • Marketing, digital, SEO, web, creative and CRM/RevOps agencies, then consultancies and other professional-services firms
    • About 8 to 50 people (sweet spot 10 to 40)
    • Several client projects running at once
    • Work split across a CRM, a project tool, email and Slack

    The buyer

    • Founder or CEO who still signs most deals
    • COO or head of operations
    • Client-services or account lead who owns onboarding

    The pain

    • What sales promised isn't what delivery hears
    • Deadlines and owners get lost between the proposal and kickoff
    • Vague words like "ongoing" or "unlimited revisions" turn into scope creep
    • Projects start late because nobody had the full picture

    Buying triggers

    • A new client kickoff went badly or a client complained about missed expectations
    • The team is growing and the founder can't be in every handoff anymore
    • They just bought a CRM or project tool and it isn't connected to how work really moves

    What success looks like for them

    • Every signed deal reaches delivery with deliverables, dates and owners named
    • Scope risks are caught before kickoff, not in month two
    • Faster time from signature to first deliverable

    How the NLP works

    A small rule-based NLP pipeline written in plain JavaScript, following the class pipeline: clean → tokenize → represent → model → evaluate.

    1. Clean: normalize spacing and quotes→ 2. Sentence split and word tokenize→ 3. Represent: stopword removal, light stemming, TF-IDF across sentences→ 4. Extract: commitment verbs, date and money patterns, role and name detection, vague-scope lexicon, sentiment lexicon→ 5. Score: readiness from what's present and what's missing
    • Deliverables are sentences with a commitment verb (deliver, build, set up, launch…) tied to a subject like "we" or a named role.
    • Dates come from patterns for calendar dates, weekdays, quarters and relative phrases like "within 3 weeks."
    • Owners come from role words (account manager, designer…) and capitalized names next to verbs like "will" or "owns."
    • Scope risks come from a lexicon of vague or open-ended language, plus checks for anything missing, such as no price or no dates.
    • Key topics use TF-IDF: words that are frequent in a few sentences but not everywhere rank highest.
